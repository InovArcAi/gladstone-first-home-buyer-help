The fine print

Three details that catch Gladstone buyers out

The residency requirement that started on 1 August 2026

From 1 August 2026, claiming any Queensland transfer duty home concession requires the buyer to be an Australian citizen, permanent resident or specified foreign retiree. A contract signed before that date is not affected; one signed after it is.

The grant is for new homes only

The $30,000 First Home Owner Grant applies to new homes under $750,000, not to established homes. An established-home buyer instead looks to the transfer duty concession and, within its cap, the 5% Deposit Scheme.

Boost to Buy does not go through a broker

Boost to Buy applications are made through Unity Bank as the approved lender. A broker can explain how the scheme compares with the alternatives, and AJ Home Loans Gladstone does exactly that in a first conversation, but the application itself is lodged with Unity Bank.

Where a broker fits

What the schemes do not cover

A grant or a concession reduces what a purchase costs. It does not decide whether a lender will lend, and it does not choose the loan. Those are separate questions, and they are the ones AJ Home Loans Gladstone works on.

Borrowing capacity

Lenders assess repayments at a buffer of 3 percentage points above the actual rate under APRA settings, which puts capacity below a simple repayment calculation.

Lender policy fit

Income type, deposit source and the property's location inside 4680 all change which lenders will write the loan on standard terms.

Loan structure

Offset, redraw, fixed and variable splits and break costs shape what the loan does for the next decade, not just its opening rate.

Timing the paperwork

A scheme deadline is only useful if the finance is ready to meet it, so document preparation starts before an offer, not after.

Common questions

What Gladstone first home buyers ask about the schemes

Am I eligible for first home buyer grants in Queensland?
Eligibility is scheme by scheme. The First Home Owner Grant applies to new homes valued under $750,000 for eligible first home buyers, the transfer duty concessions apply to first homes within their thresholds, and the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme has carried no income caps since 1 October 2025. Each scheme's own eligibility page is linked in the table on this page.
How much deposit do I need?
It depends on the lender and the scheme. Under the Australian Government 5% Deposit Scheme an eligible first home buyer can purchase with a 5% deposit and no Lenders Mortgage Insurance, within the $700,000 property price cap that applies to Gladstone. Outside the scheme, a deposit under 20% usually attracts Lenders Mortgage Insurance, and each lender sets its own minimums.
What happens if interest rates change again?
Rates move and no one can promise where they go next. The Reserve Bank of Australia held the cash rate at 4.35% at its meeting on 11 August 2026. The grants and concessions on this page are set by legislation rather than by the cash rate, so a rate change does not remove them, but your borrowing capacity is assessed at a buffer above the actual rate.
Can these schemes be combined?
Some can and some cannot. The First Home Owner Grant and a transfer duty concession can apply to the same eligible purchase, while the vacant land concession rules out claiming the new home concession later on that land. Boost to Buy runs through Unity Bank directly rather than through a broker. Which combination applies depends on the property and the contract date.
